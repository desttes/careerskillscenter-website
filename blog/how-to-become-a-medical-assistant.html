How to Become a Medical Assistant: 4 Routes, Real Costs and the Certificate Each Leads To

Some employers pay you to train, and some cheap courses limit which certificate you can take. Here are the four routes, with real times, costs and the hard parts.

Published October 5, 202611 min read

Most guides on how to become a medical assistant tell you to pick a school first. Pick your route first instead.

Some employers will pay you while they train you. A cheap online course can get you working fast, but it may lock you out of one of the main certificates.

This guide shows the four routes side by side, with real times and costs. Career Skills Center does not sell medical assistant training, so we can be straight with you.

Quick answer: 5 steps to become a medical assistant
  1. Finish high school or get your GED.
  2. Pick your route: paid employer training, a college program, a short program, or a job that trains you.
  3. Train for about 8 weeks to 2 years, depending on the route.
  4. Get certified. Most states don’t require it by law, but many employers want it.
  5. Find your first job. It can be the hardest step, so plan for it early.

What does a medical assistant do?

A medical assistant helps doctors and nurses run a clinic. You work in the exam room and at the front desk, often on the same day.

A normal day might start with taking a patient’s vital signs, like blood pressure and pulse. Later you might book follow-up visits or update medical records.

  • Taking vital signs and health histories
  • Getting patients ready for exams
  • Collecting samples for lab tests
  • Giving medicines, when a provider directs you
  • Scheduling visits and keeping records up to date

More than half of medical assistants work in doctors’ offices. Most work full time and in person, though some clinics are open evenings or weekends.

To compare other options, see our guide to healthcare jobs you can train for.

What you need to start

To start, you need a high school diploma or GED. You do not need any work experience.

Programs and employers may also ask for a background check, a physical, shot records and a TB test. Some want a CPR card (Basic Life Support).

Some programs require you to be 18 or older. If you have a criminal record, ask the program and the certifying group how it is reviewed before you pay.

The route decides what you pay, whether you are paid while you learn, and which certificate you can take. So choose it before you choose a school.

Route A: Paid employer training or apprenticeship

Some hospitals and health systems hire beginners and pay them while they train. Some call it a registered apprenticeship: a paid job plus classes.

For example, Fred Hutch Cancer Center in Seattle pays apprentices full time for 2,000 hours of on-the-job training. It also covers the $4,850 tuition.

Its 410 hours of online coursework are unpaid and done on your own time. After the program, you agree to work there for 2 more years.

Fred Hutch expects its next group to start in January 2027 (as of October 2026). Check current enrollment with the program.

Kaiser Permanente Washington runs a similar paid program: 2,000 hours on the job over 12 to 24 months, plus 288 hours of classes.

As of October 2026, Kaiser asks for 2 years of work after the program. Check current enrollment with the program.

Spots in these programs are few. Before you sign, ask what happens if you leave early.

There is also a back door. Some health systems train their own staff first, so a front-desk or other non-clinical job there can lead to their medical assistant training.

Route B: A college program

A college certificate or diploma teaches you in class, in a lab and in a real clinic. It takes about 8 months to 2 years.

This route keeps every door open. A program accredited (checked and approved) by CAAHEP or ABHES can lead to any of the four main certificates.

You can look programs up on the CAAHEP and ABHES lists. The AAMA also currently accepts some other programs at accredited colleges through its Alternative Pathway.

That program needs at least 560 class hours plus a 160-hour externship (or 1,000 hours of work after). You must also give at least 10 shots and do at least 10 blood draws.

For example, Central Piedmont Community College in North Carolina has a CAAHEP-accredited diploma that takes about 2.5 semesters.

As of October 2026, it lists total costs of $5,376 to $14,400. Check current enrollment with the program.

An externship is hands-on practice in a real clinic near the end of a program. It is usually unpaid and during the day.

Route C: A short or online program

Short programs can teach the basics in as little as about 8 weeks, and many are online. They are the fastest way to finish training.

The trade-off is the certificate. A short program usually leads to the CCMA, because it often won’t meet the hour rules for the CMA (AAMA) or the RMA.

For example, Community College of Philadelphia’s non-credit course costs $2,799 for 180 hours. It runs about 8 weeks by day or 16 weeks in the evening.

Anne Arundel Community College in Maryland charges county residents $4,112 for 388 hours. That includes a 100-hour unpaid externship, and graduates can take the CCMA exam.

These prices are as of October 2026. Check current enrollment with the program.

Watch out for programs with no hands-on practice. Some students say you can’t learn shots and blood draws through a screen, so look for in-person labs or an externship.

Route D: Get hired and learn on the job

Some clinics hire people with a diploma or GED and train them on the job over several months. You are paid while you learn.

Later, that work can count toward a certificate. One year of supervised work in the last 3 years (or 2 years in the last 5) can qualify you for the CCMA.

Three years of full-time work in the last 7 can qualify you for the RMA. There is no work-only route to the CMA (AAMA).

There is a catch. Some people who were hired with no training were later told to get certified.

So when you get a job offer, ask these questions:

  • Will I need a certificate?
  • By when?
  • Will you pay for the exam?
Before you pay for any program, ask:
  • Is it accredited, and by which group?
  • Which certification exam does it prepare me for? Is the exam fee in the price?
  • How many externship hours are there, who finds the site, and what are the hours?
  • What is the full price in writing, with books, scrubs, exam and background check?

How long does it take to become a medical assistant?

For most people, it takes months, not years. A short program can take about 8 weeks, and most college certificates take 8 months to a year.

An associate degree takes 2 years, and paid apprenticeships take 1 to 2 years. Then plan time for the exam and your job search.

How much does it cost?

Paid training often costs you nothing. The public college programs above cost about $2,800 to $14,400, and private school prices vary a lot.

The exam adds $125 to $250. Budget for scrubs, books, a background check and a CPR card too.

Public training money may help if you qualify. Find out whether WIOA training is really free.

Can you train while working?

You can do most of the training while you work. Many programs are hybrid, with classes online and labs in person, and some offer evening classes.

The externship is the hard part. At one Maryland college, it is 25 hours a week for 4 weeks, Monday to Friday, so plan time off from your job.

Which certification should you get? CMA, RMA, CCMA or CMAC

A certification is a national credential you earn by passing an exam. Many employers prefer or require one.

Your route decides which exam you can take. Check the job ads in your area to see which certificates employers ask for.

How hard is it to become a medical assistant?

The classes

You learn medical words, body systems, basic dosage math, and how to give shots and draw blood. Plan study time every week.

If drawing blood is the part you like best, read how to become a phlebotomist.

The exam

About 7 in 10 people pass the CMA (AAMA) on their first try. You need a score of 405 on a scale of 200 to 800.

If you don’t pass, you can try again. The RMA, for example, allows up to 4 tries, at least 45 days apart.

The first job

This can be the hardest step. Some people are hired at the site where they did their externship.

Others wait months for an employer who will take a beginner. So treat your externship like a long job interview, and start looking for jobs before you finish.

The work

Many medical assistants like the work and say they make a difference for patients. Some say the pay and the workload make it hard to stay for a whole career.

If you are not sure this job is for you, explore our career paths.

Pay, and is it worth it?

Most medical assistants earn around $45,690 a year. New medical assistants often start around $36,050.

The number of medical assistant jobs is expected to grow 13% over the next 10 years, with about 109,700 openings a year.

Pay in your area may be higher or lower, so read local job ads before you pay for school. It can be worth it if you keep your training cost low.

If you hope to become a nurse later, ask the nursing school which classes it will count. General classes, like English or biology, are the most likely to count.

Rules are different in some states

Most states have no license for medical assistants. A few have their own rules, so check yours before you choose a route.

For example, Washington requires a state credential before you can work as a medical assistant. The certified one costs $145 and needs approved training plus a national exam.

Its on-the-job route, the registered credential, costs $115. It only counts while you work for the same employer.

To check your state, see the AAMA’s state-by-state page or ask your state medical board.

If you live in Massachusetts

No state license required. Massachusetts does not license medical assistants, and certification is up to each employer.

One state law matters. A primary care provider may let a medical assistant give immunizations (vaccines), but only under direct supervision.

The provider must be in the building and ready to help. You must work in that practice and have finished a CAAHEP- or ABHES-accredited program, so on-the-job training alone does not count.

Public programs in Massachusetts

Here are a few public options, as of October 2026. Check current enrollment with each program.

  • Greenfield Community College: CAAHEP-accredited, about 8 months, mostly online with in-person labs.
  • Massasoit Community College: 9 months, full time by day, with a 245-hour clinical practicum.
  • Quincy College: 12 weeks of hybrid classes plus a 3-week internship, with evening and weekend options. Spring 2027 groups start in January and February.
  • Quinsigamond Community College and UMass Memorial Health: a paid apprenticeship for employees, with 2,000 hours on the job and 8 weeks of classes.

Help paying in Massachusetts

MassEducate may cover community college tuition and fees for eligible students. You need at least 6 credits a term, so short non-credit programs may not count.

A MassHire Career Center may help pay if you qualify and the program is on the state’s training list. Learn how a MassHire training voucher works.

Frequently asked questions

Can you become a medical assistant online?

You can take most of the classes online. But you still need hands-on practice with shots and blood draws, in a lab and an externship.

If you want an office-only health job, read whether medical billing and coding can be learned online.

Will AI replace medical assistants?

No one can say for sure. Much of the job is hands-on work with patients, like taking vital signs and collecting samples for lab tests.

Medical assistant jobs are still expected to grow 13% over the next 10 years, mostly because more older people need care.

Is a medical assistant the same as a physician assistant?

No, they are different jobs. A physician assistant needs a master's degree and a state license, and can diagnose and treat patients.

A medical assistant can start after as little as about 8 weeks of training and does not diagnose, prescribe or treat.

Medical assistant or CNA: which pays more?

Medical assistants earn a little more. Most earn around $45,690 a year, compared with about $42,260 for nursing assistants.

Read how to become a CNA to compare the two jobs.

Do you have to be certified to work as a medical assistant?

In most states, no law requires it. But many employers prefer or require a certificate, so plan to get one.
