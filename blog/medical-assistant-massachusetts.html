How to Become a Medical Assistant in Massachusetts (2026): Certification, Pay and Paid Training

Massachusetts has no license for medical assistants. Here are the three ways in, including one that pays you to train, which certification each leads to, and the hard parts of the job.

Published October 5, 202613 min read

Most guides to becoming a medical assistant in Massachusetts start with a list of schools. Start here instead.

Some Massachusetts health centers and hospitals will pay you while they train you. If you pay for school yourself, the school you pick decides which certification you can earn.

This guide shows the three ways in, what each costs you and the hard parts of the job. Career Skills Center does not sell medical assistant training, so we can be straight with you.

Quick answer.
  • No state license required. Many employers prefer or require a national certification.
  • There are three ways in: an employer trains you, an accredited program, or a shorter program that is not accredited.
  • Most medical assistants earn around $45,690 a year.
  • The hardest part for many people is getting the first job, so plan for it.

Words to know

  • Medical assistant: a person who helps doctors and nurses in a clinic, with patient care and office work. It is not the same job as a physician assistant.
  • Vital signs: basic body checks, like blood pressure, pulse and temperature.
  • Externship: hands-on practice in a real clinic near the end of a program. It is often unpaid.
  • Accredited program: a program checked and approved by an outside group. For medical assistants, the two main groups are CAAHEP and ABHES.
  • Certification: a national credential you earn by passing an exam. The main ones are the CMA (Certified Medical Assistant), RMA (Registered Medical Assistant) and CCMA (Certified Clinical Medical Assistant).
  • Immunization: a shot that protects against a disease, like a flu shot.
  • CORI (Criminal Offender Record Information): the Massachusetts criminal background check.

What is a medical assistant?

A medical assistant helps doctors and nurses run a clinic. You do some patient care and some office work, often on the same day.

This mix matters when you choose the job. You need to be good with people and also comfortable with records and scheduling.

A normal day might start with bringing a patient to the exam room and taking their blood pressure. Later you might answer phones or book follow-up visits.

Patient care tasks can include:

  • Taking vital signs and asking about health history
  • Getting patients ready for exams and helping the doctor
  • Collecting samples for lab tests
  • Giving medicines, when allowed and supervised

Office tasks can include:

  • Scheduling appointments and answering phones
  • Keeping medical records up to date
  • Helping with insurance and billing paperwork

More than half of medical assistants work in doctors’ offices. Others work in hospitals and outpatient clinics.

Most work full time. Some clinics are open evenings, weekends or holidays, so ask about the schedule.

Some medical assistants say the job changes a lot from office to office. Some offices keep you in the exam room, and some move you to the front desk when the doctor is out.

In a job interview, ask: “What would I do on a normal day?” The answer tells you which kind of office it is.

Do you need a license to be a medical assistant in Massachusetts?

No state license required. Massachusetts does not license medical assistants.

Certification is different. No state law requires it, but many employers prefer or require it, so plan for it.

The one state rule: giving shots

Massachusetts has one rule to know before you pick a school. It is about giving shots (immunizations).

A primary care office may let a medical assistant give shots only if that person finished an accredited program.

A primary care provider, such as a doctor or nurse practitioner, must be in the building and ready to help.

The program must be accredited by CAAHEP or ABHES, or approved by the state health department. If you want to give shots, ask the school which group accredits it.

Other tasks depend on the employer and the supervising provider. Ask each employer what medical assistants are allowed to do there.

Three ways to become a medical assistant

There is more than one way in. An employer can train you, you can finish an accredited program, or you can take a shorter program that is not accredited.

The route you pick changes your cost, your time and which certification you can earn. It also decides whether you can give shots later.

For example, some Massachusetts health centers hire beginners and pay them an hourly wage while they train. Some also pay the tuition.

What do I need to start?

To start, you usually need a high school diploma or GED. You do not need work experience.

College programs may also ask for a background (CORI) check, a physical exam and your shot records.

Some employers also want CPR (Basic Life Support) certification. Ask each program and employer what they need before you start.

How long does it take?

Most programs take from a few months to 2 years. Shorter programs are often not accredited, so check what you give up before you choose speed.

Training is not the whole timeline. After training, you take a certification exam and look for your first job, and that can take time too.

Can you train while working? Many college programs are hybrid, with classes mostly online and labs in person.

Some programs have evening classes. The externship is often during the day and unpaid, so ask about the schedule early.

Can you do it fully online? Some people say a program with no hands-on practice hurt their job search.

Online classes are fine. The problem is a program with no hands-on practice at all, because two of the three main certifications require hands-on training.

CMA, RMA or CCMA: which certification can you get?

A certification is a national credential you earn by passing an exam. It works the same in every state, so you deal with the group that gives it, not the state.

Your training decides which exams you can take. Check this before you pay, not after.

For example, the CMA needs an accredited program. A short program that is not accredited will generally not get you there.

Rules change, so check the group’s own page: AAMA (CMA), AMT (RMA) or NHA (CCMA).

Is the exam hard to pass?

About 7 in 10 people pass the CMA on their first try. Many people pass, but you should still plan real study time.

Each exam is timed. Ask your program which exam it prepares you for, and whether the exam fee is in the price.

If you have a criminal record

A felony record can affect whether you may take the CMA or RMA exam. It is not an automatic ban, because each case is reviewed one by one.

For the CMA, a drug-related conviction can block a waiver. For the CCMA, check with NHA before you pay for training.

Community college programs may also run a CORI check. Ask the program and the certifying group how a record is reviewed before you pay.

How much do medical assistants make?

Most medical assistants earn around $45,690 a year. New medical assistants often start lower.

Pay depends on where you work, such as a doctor’s office, a hospital or an outpatient clinic.

The number of medical assistant jobs in the U.S. is expected to grow 13% over the next 10 years. There are about 109,700 openings each year.

Medical assistant or CNA?

Both are healthcare jobs you can train for with a high school diploma or GED. But the rules in Massachusetts are very different.

A CNA must pass a state exam and be listed on the state Nurse Aide Registry. A medical assistant needs no state license.

The hard truth: getting hired and staying

The jobs are there, but the first one is often the hardest to get. Some people say this was the hardest step, even after they finished a program and passed a certification.

Employers often ask for experience, even for beginner jobs. So plan your job search before you finish training.

There is another side. Some clinics hire people with no certification and train them, and some people say more doors open after that first job.

Some people say their first job came through their externship site, a classmate who was already hired, or a contact at the clinic.

So treat your externship like a long job interview. Let people there know you want to stay.

Why people leave, and why they stay

Medical assistants who leave often talk about pay, short staffing, poor bosses and not feeling appreciated. Some say raises are small.

Some who want much more money go back to school for nursing. Moving up usually takes more training.

Many others stay, and many like their work. They talk about their coworkers, their patients and good doctors.

These are personal experiences, not promises about any job. The office you pick makes a big difference.

Will AI replace medical assistants?

No one can say for sure. But much of the job is hands-on with patients, like taking vital signs, and that needs a person.

Office tasks, like scheduling and records, are more likely to change. Medical assistant jobs are still expected to grow 13% over the next 10 years.

Can you work from home?

Most of this job is done in person, because it involves patient care. Remote jobs, when you find them, are usually office work like scheduling or records.

Is this job for you?

This job may fit you if:

  • You like working with people, including sick or worried patients.
  • You want variety: some patient care and some office work.
  • You want a way into healthcare without years of school.

It is probably not for you if:

  • Needles, blood or body fluids bother you.
  • You want less hands-on patient care. Read how to become a pharmacy technician in Massachusetts.
  • You want work outside healthcare. Explore our career paths.

Is it worth it? It can be, if you avoid big debt and see it as a first step into healthcare.

Before you pay for a program, ask these questions

Write down the answers and compare at least two options. A good program will answer every question clearly.

Questions to ask
  1. Is the school licensed, and is the program accredited by CAAHEP or ABHES?
  2. Which certification exam does it prepare me for? Is the exam fee included?
  3. Where will I practice hands-on skills? Is there an externship, and who finds the site?
  4. What is the full price in writing, with fees, books, exam, background check and scrubs?
  5. What days and times are classes, labs and the externship?
  6. Do you run a CORI check? How is a record reviewed?
  7. Do the credits count toward nursing later?
  8. Is the program on the state’s list of eligible training programs (ETPL), if I want MassHire help?
  9. If an employer pays: what happens if I leave early?

To check accreditation, use the links to the CAAHEP and ABHES program lists.

How to pay for medical assistant training in Massachusetts

You have more options than paying the full price yourself. Start with the ones that cost you the least.

Employer-paid training

Some Massachusetts health employers pay you while you train and also cover tuition. Ask health centers and hospitals near you if they train new medical assistants.

Read any work agreement before you sign. Ask what happens if you leave early.

Free community college

Through MassEducate, eligible students may get full tuition and fees covered for a community college certificate.

You must take at least 6 credits a term and fill out a financial aid form (FAFSA or MASFA). Some short programs carry no credits, so ask the college if yours counts.

MassHire and WIOA

A MassHire Career Center may help pay for training if you qualify. The program must be on the state’s list of eligible training programs (ETPL).

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a license to be a medical assistant in Massachusetts?

No state license required. Massachusetts does not license medical assistants.

Many employers prefer or require a national certification, like the CMA, RMA or CCMA. To give shots in a primary care office, you must finish an accredited program.

How long does it take to become a medical assistant?

Most programs take from a few months to 2 years. Some employers train you on the job, which often takes several months.

After training, plan extra time for the certification exam and your job search.

Is the medical assistant exam hard?

About 7 in 10 people pass the CMA exam on their first try. It has 200 questions and takes about 2 hours and 40 minutes.

The RMA exam has 210 questions in 2 hours, and the CCMA exam takes 3 hours. Plan real study time.

Can a medical assistant give shots in Massachusetts?

Yes, in a primary care office, if you finished an accredited program and a primary care provider supervises you. That can be a doctor or nurse practitioner, who must be in the building.

Other tasks depend on the employer, so ask what medical assistants are allowed to do there.

What do I need to start medical assistant training?

You usually need a high school diploma or GED. You do not need work experience.

College programs may also ask for a CORI check, a physical exam and your shot records. Some employers also want CPR (Basic Life Support) certification.

How much does medical assistant training cost, and can I get it free?

Prices vary by school. A certificate at a public college can cost several thousand dollars, before books and exam fees.

Some employers pay you while you train. You may also qualify for free community college or for help from a MassHire Career Center.

Is a medical assistant the same as a CNA?

No. A medical assistant works mostly in doctors' offices and clinics, does patient care and office work, and needs no state license.

A CNA gives daily care, mostly in nursing homes and hospitals. A CNA must pass a state exam and be on the Nurse Aide Registry.
