Medical Coding and Billing Salary by State (2026): What Coders and Billers Earn

Medical coders earn a median of $51,140 a year ($24.59 an hour). See pay in every state, what beginners make, and whether coders or billers earn more.

Published October 1, 2026. 7 min read

Medical coders earn a median of $51,140 a year, or $24.59 an hour, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (May 2025). Most earn between $37,000 and $81,150. Where you live, whether you're certified and where you work all make a big difference.

Below you'll find pay at every level, what beginners make, whether coders or billers earn more, and pay in every state.

How much money do you make as a medical coder?

Half of all medical coders earn more than $51,140 a year, and half earn less. The government counts coders under the job title "medical records specialists," which also includes some records and health information workers.

Here is the full pay range for May 2025:

The average (mean) was a bit higher, $56,790, because a smaller group of experienced coders earns well above the median.

How much can a beginner coder make?

Expect to start somewhere around $37,000 to $43,500 a year, or about $18 to $21 an hour. The government doesn't publish pay by years of experience, but new workers usually earn near the lower end of the range, between the lowest 10 percent and the lower 25 percent in the table above.

A few things can move your starting pay up:

  • Where you live. Starting pay in a high-wage state can be higher than the national median in a low-wage state. See the state table below.
  • Getting certified. Many employers prefer or require a certification such as the CPC. If you pass without work experience, you start as a CPC-A (apprentice), which some employers hire specifically.
  • Starting in a related job. Many coders begin in billing, patient registration or the front desk, then move into coding at the same employer.

If you're still deciding how to train, our guide on 4-week vs. 4-month medical billing and coding courses compares the time and total cost of each path.

What pays more, medical coding or billing?

Coding usually pays a little more. In May 2025:

Median pay, U.S., May 2025
JobPer yearPer hour
Medical records specialists (includes medical coders)$51,140$24.59
Billing and posting clerks (includes medical billers)$48,500$23.32

Two things to keep in mind:

  • The billing number isn't only medical. The government's "billing and posting clerks" group includes billing jobs in every industry, not just healthcare.
  • Many jobs do both. In smaller medical offices, one person often codes visits and sends the claims. Job titles like "medical billing and coding specialist" are common, and they usually pay in the coder range.

Certification shows a similar pattern. In AAPC's 2026 salary report, members holding the Certified Professional Coder (CPC) credential averaged $67,147 in 2025.

What raises your pay

Certification. This is the biggest factor most people can control. In AAPC's 2026 salary report, certified members averaged $67,260, compared with $55,721 for members without a certification, a difference of about 21 percent. Members with two AAPC certifications averaged $74,557, and those with three or more averaged $81,227.

AAPC's figures come from a survey of its own members, who tend to be more experienced than the average coder. That's why they run higher than the government numbers above. Use them to compare certified and non-certified pay, not as a starting salary.

Experience and specialty. After a few years, coders often move into specialty coding, auditing or compliance, which usually pay more.

Location. Moving to a higher-paying state or landing a remote job based in one can raise your pay, though higher costs of living can eat into it.

Do medical coders get paid well?

Medical coding pays close to the middle of the U.S. job market. The median for medical records specialists ($51,140) is just above the median for all U.S. jobs ($50,980). What makes it attractive to many people is the combination: you can train in months rather than years, there's no state license, and experienced, certified coders can earn well above the median.

Demand looks steady too. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects jobs for medical records specialists to grow about 8 percent from 2025 to 2035, much faster than the average for all occupations.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do you make as a medical coder?

The median pay for medical coders and other medical records specialists was $51,140 a year, or $24.59 an hour, in May 2025, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. Most earn between $37,000 and $81,150.

How much can a beginner medical coder make?

The government doesn't track pay by experience, but the lower end of the pay range is a good guide. A quarter of medical records specialists earned $43,490 or less in May 2025, and the lowest 10 percent earned under $37,000. That's about $18 to $21 an hour.

Who gets paid more, a medical biller or a medical coder?

Coders usually earn a little more. Medical records specialists, the group that includes coders, had a median of $51,140 in May 2025. Billing and posting clerks had a median of $48,500. Many jobs combine both tasks.

Do medical coders get paid well?

Medical coding pays close to the middle of the U.S. job market. The median for medical records specialists ($51,140) is just above the median for all jobs ($50,980). Certification tends to raise pay: AAPC's 2026 report found certified members averaged $67,260, compared with $55,721 for members without a certification.

Which state pays medical coders the most?

In May 2025 the highest medians were in the District of Columbia, Rhode Island, Hawaii, Washington and California, all above $61,000 a year.
