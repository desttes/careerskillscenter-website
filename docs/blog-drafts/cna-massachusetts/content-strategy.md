## Content Gaps Found
- **"Will I have to pay for training?" The federal rule nobody explains.** Under 42 CFR 483.152(c) (search summary;
  verify on eCFR), a nurse aide who is **employed by, or has a job offer from, a nursing facility** when training starts
  **cannot be charged** for any part of the training and competency evaluation, including textbooks. If you pay yourself
  and a nursing facility hires you **within 12 months** of finishing, the **state must reimburse** your costs pro rata
  while you work there. No top result states this plainly. It changes the reader's decision: "get hired first, then
  trained" can cost $0. **Researcher must confirm:** the eCFR text, and how Massachusetts actually runs the reimbursement
  (a search summary of a 2023 bill, H.1216, describes a DPH reimbursement program; a bill is not law, so find the real
  DPH process or say "ask DPH").
- **"Free" training with strings.** Free nursing-home training is real, but job postings (seen only as snippets) show
  terms like "work full time for 2 years or repay the class." Nobody tells readers to read the contract first. Name no
  employer in the post.
- **The 87-hour change.** A DPH/EOHHS advisory memo dated **April 3, 2026** (summary only) raises the state minimum from
  the federal 75 hours to **87 hours, including 21 hours of supervised practical training**, with a new state curriculum
  framework, free state online learning modules (40+ hours, in English, Spanish and Haitian Creole) planned for 2026, and a
  **revised state exam expected early 2027**. Current CNAs in good standing do not retrain. Every competitor still says
  75 hours. **Researcher must confirm the effective date** (the existing VERIFICATION_LOG N3 already flags "87-hour
  minimum, effective date unconfirmed"). If the date is not confirmed, say "Massachusetts is raising the minimum; ask the
  program how many hours it is."
- **Exam cost is no longer free.** D&S announced (summary only) that starting **July 1, 2025** the first-time exam is no
  longer free for candidates not registered by their trainer by that date. Retake fees in the Oct 2025 handbook:
  knowledge **$30**, audio knowledge **$40**, skills **$70** (handbook). First-attempt fees did not extract from the
  handbook: researcher to read the May 2026 handbook. Also (handbook): "Some training programs pre-pay testing fees,"
  so the honest tip is "ask the program if the exam fee is included."
- **What the exam really looks like** (handbook, Oct 2025): 60 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes, 76% to pass;
  audio (oral) version available; knowledge test can be taken remotely from home with a proctor; skills test is 3 or 4
  randomly chosen tasks in 40 minutes, the first always includes hand washing; up to **4 knowledge tries and 3 skills
  tries**, then you must retake a DPH-approved training program; "training does not expire." Competitors get the time,
  task count and attempts wrong.
- **Languages.** Since July 15, 2024 the exam is offered in Spanish and Chinese; Haitian Creole added December 2, 2024
  (D&S home page). **Conflict:** the Oct 2025 handbook's language paragraph lists only English, Spanish and Chinese.
  Researcher must confirm Haitian Creole is still offered before we print it. Also (handbook): you must test in the
  language you picked at registration and cannot switch mid-way.
- **ID rules that trip up immigrants** (handbook): you need an original, unexpired, **U.S. government-issued** photo ID
  with a signature. A **foreign passport is not accepted** unless it has a U.S. visa attached. A Green Card or Employment
  Authorization Card **is** accepted. Your first and last name must match exactly what the training program entered.
  No competitor mentions this. A wrong ID means a no-show and lost fees.
- **Criminal record (CORI).** Nursing homes and home care agencies must run CORI checks. Under 101 CMR 15.00, some offenses
  mean automatic disqualification and others get an individual review; EOHHS policy says rehabilitated people should get
  a fair chance (search summaries; verify). No top guide covers it. For our readers this is often the first question.
  Give no legal advice: say what the rule is, link it, and point to MassHire and free legal help for record sealing.
- **Working before you're certified.** Federal rules (search summary; verify 42 CFR 483.35) let a nursing facility use a
  new aide for up to 4 months while they train and test. This is the "earn while you learn" path. Nobody explains it.
- **Renewal** (handbook): renew every 24 months; you must have worked **for pay** as a nurse aide at least 8 consecutive
  hours in those 24 months. People who train and then don't work lose eligibility. Only some competitors mention it.
- **Reciprocity** (handbook): CNAs current and in good standing in another state can apply. Useful for movers; covered
  by nursinglicensure.org only.

## Structural Opportunity
Every guide is either a ranked school list or the same "Step 1-4" template. Missing:
1. **"The real path, start to finish" numbered checklist** with an honest time note on each step (find a program or an
   employer who will train you → background check and health tests → training → schedule the exam → pass both parts →
   registry listing → keep it active). Flag which step usually takes longest. End with outward next steps (MassHire
   Career Center, JobQuest).
2. **A "3 ways to pay" comparison table:** (a) employer-paid (nursing home hires or offers you a job first), (b) public
   free programs (MassHire / workforce boards, city and community-college grant programs, often with eligibility rules),
   (c) pay yourself, then possible reimbursement if a nursing facility hires you within 12 months. Columns: cost to you,
   strings attached, who it fits.
3. **"Exam day at a glance" box** from the handbook: parts, time, passing score, tries, languages, audio option, ID rule.
4. **"Before you sign up, ask these 7 questions" box:** Is the program DPH-approved (check the state list)? How many hours
   (87 or more under the new rule)? Is the exam fee included? Where is the clinical site and what hours? Is there a
   work contract, and what happens if I leave? What English level do I need, and is there an ESL track? Do you help me
   get a job?
5. **"Is this job for you?" yes/no list** and a short **CNA vs. home health aide vs. phlebotomist vs. pharmacy tech** box
   linking our other posts.
No school rankings or directories.

## Freshness Opportunity
- **Training hours:** all competitors say 75; the April 2026 DPH memo says 87 (21 supervised practical). Biggest
  freshness win if the effective date is confirmed.
- **Exam:** revised state exam expected early 2027 (memo, summary only). First exam not free since July 1, 2025 (D&S,
  summary only); nursinglicensure.org (updated June 2026) still says free.
- **Pay:** dreambound uses **May 2024** BLS; cnaclasses.org uses 2024 metro data; cnaonlinecourse.com says "$16/hour."
  BLS OEWS **May 2025** is out: national median for SOC 31-1131 **$42,260** (VERIFICATION_LOG N1) and Massachusetts
  median **$46,680** (VERIFICATION_LOG H1; a third-party search summary matched it). The 10th percentile is the honest
  "starting" signal; take it from BLS only (the N1 row is flagged "confirm which row").
  **POLICY FLAG (still open):** PROJECT-HANDOFF.md allows pay figures only in the salary-by-state post. This is the same
  open question as the healthcare-jobs, phlebotomist and pharmacy posts. Fallback: link to the BLS page and the
  Massachusetts OEWS table instead of printing numbers.
- **Outlook:** **Conflict.** VERIFICATION_LOG N2 holds **3% (2025-35)** for CNAs; search summaries and dreambound show
  **2% (2024-34)** for "nursing assistants and orderlies" with about 209,400 openings a year. These may be different
  vintages or different rows. Researcher must confirm the current BLS figure for nursing assistants live.
- **Languages and remote testing:** exam languages (2024) and remote-proctored knowledge test are recent and barely
  covered.

## Honesty Opportunity
- **"Online CNA classes."** Two ranking pages lead with "online." Theory can be online (and the state's free modules will
  be), but clinical skills and the skills test are in person.
- **"Done in 3-4 weeks."** Classroom maybe. Add the exam date wait, results, registry listing, and (for many) the hiring
  steps: CORI, TB test, immunizations, physical.
- **"Free training!"** Often true, often with a work contract. We say: read it, ask what happens if you leave early.
- **Wrong exam facts** repeated across the SERP (5 skills, 2 hours, $100-$115, 3 tries in 2 years). We use only the D&S
  handbook and DPH.
- **Pay hype:** uncited "$3,000-$10,000 sign-on bonuses," city averages from aggregators. We use BLS medians (if
  approved), say the median is the middle, not a starting wage.
- **Hidden downsides** (source from BLS OOH "Work Environment" only; verify live): one of the highest injury and illness
  rates of any job (lifting and moving people), nights/weekends/holidays, short staffing, heavy emotional load including
  residents dying, and slow pay growth. Growth usually means more school (for example LPN or RN). Also: the registry
  lapses if you don't work paid aide hours.
- **"License."** Several pages call it a CNA "license." In Massachusetts you are certified and listed on the DPH Nurse
  Aide Registry. Small, but it shows we know the system.

## Audience Opportunity
- **Working adults with a GED, low income, Black Americans, immigrants.** The SERP is written for someone shopping for a
  school with money ready. Our reader wants: Can I do this without paying? Can I keep working? Will my record stop me?
  Can I test in my language? What ID do I need? Nobody answers these together.
- **ESL readers.** Exam in Spanish/Chinese (and Haitian Creole, if confirmed), audio option, ESL-track programs exist
  (for example the Cambridge city program has an English-learner track; describe as an example of what exists, dated,
  not as an offer). Glossary: registry, competency evaluation, clinical hours, CORI, reciprocity, resident.
- **People with a record.** CORI section, plain and careful, no promises.
- **People who need funding.** Employer-paid first, then MassHire Career Center (WIOA training funds; "you may qualify";
  check the MA ETPL), then pay-and-reimburse. Never promise approval. No funding amounts unless from
  `js/site-config.js`.
- **Explorers.** "Is this job for you?" list and a comparison box linking our phlebotomist and pharmacy tech posts.

## RECOMMENDED ANGLE
**Angle:** The up-to-date, no-sales Massachusetts CNA guide that answers the money question first: you may not have to
pay for training at all, plus the 2026 changes (87 hours, exam no longer free, new exam in 2027) and the practical
barriers other guides skip (background checks, ID rules, test language).

**Hook:** Open on the money, in plain words. Example direction: "Most CNA guides start with a list of schools and a
price tag. Start here instead: in Massachusetts, if a nursing home hires you or offers you a job before your training
starts, federal rules say it cannot charge you for that training. That is one of three ways to pay for CNA training, and
the cheapest. This guide shows all three, how the state exam and registry work, what changed in 2026, and the hard parts
of the job. We don't sell CNA training, so we can be straight with you."
(Writer: hook facts depend on the researcher confirming 42 CFR 483.152 live. Keep the last line accurate to guide mode.
Any "we plan to offer healthcare training" line must use the allowed wording and be marked COURSE-DEPENDENT.)

**Key differentiators:**
1. **"3 ways to pay" section and table**, built on the federal no-charge rule and the 12-month reimbursement rule
   (eCFR + DPH), public free programs via MassHire, and a clear warning about work contracts. No competitor has this.
2. **Current rules, from the state and its test vendor only:** 87-hour minimum (if the date is confirmed), exam-at-a-
   glance box (60 questions, 60 minutes, 76%, 3-4 skills tasks, 4/3 tries, audio and remote options, languages), exam no
   longer free, 2027 exam change, renewal (8 paid hours in 24 months), reciprocity. Fixes the wrong facts repeated across
   the SERP.
3. **The barriers our readers actually hit:** CORI (101 CMR 15.00, careful and linked), the ID rule (foreign passport
   not accepted without a U.S. visa; Green Card/EAD accepted; names must match), testing in Spanish/Chinese, and an
   honest "is this job for you?" list (injury risk, nights and weekends, emotional load, slow pay growth, where it leads).

**Why this wins:** Readers: every long guide ranking now earns money when you enroll; the two honest public pages are
local only. Ours is the one page that tells a low-income reader how to get trained for $0, what can stop them, and what
the job is really like, with no pitch. Google: it answers the question-style searches directly (FAQPage schema), uses
newer official data than every competitor (April 2026 DPH memo, D&S handbook, May 2025 OEWS, current projections), cites
primary sources (eCFR, DPH, D&S, BLS, mass.gov), and adds unique structured content (pay-paths table, exam box,
checklist). That is the people-first profile that beats directory templates.

**Suggested title:** "How to Become a CNA in Massachusetts (2026): Training, State Exam, Registry and How to Get
Training Paid For" (trim to fit; keep "CNA" and "Massachusetts" early).
