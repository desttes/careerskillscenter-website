## C1. Skills test (steps, say aloud, comments, challenge)

Source for all items: May 2026 handbook (URL above) [OPENED].

1. **Missing one critical step fails the skill: CONSISTENT.** Handbook, Skills Test: "You must correctly perform all critical steps (in bold font) and 80% of all non-key steps on each task assigned to pass the Skill Test." Also: "You must score 80% on each scenario without missing any critical steps (the bolded steps)." Note the 80% applies per task, so one failed task fails the skills test.
2. **"Say every step out loud": CONTRADICTED as a general rule. Do not print it.** Handbook: "All steps must actually be demonstrated. Steps that are only verbalized WILL NOT COUNT." Verbalizing is required only for a few named steps (soaking time for foot and nail soak: "You MUST verbalize the 5 to 20 minutes of soaking time"; "at least 5 minutes" for nails). The handbook also says to verbalize "I'm finished" after each task, and that it is "helpful" to verbalize separating the labia. Safe wording for the post: "Do every step with your hands. A few steps, like soak time, you must also say aloud. Tell the observer when you finish each task." The Reddit tip (talk through steps) is a community habit, not a handbook rule. Handbook also lets you correct yourself: "tell the RN Test Observer you would like to make a correction" at any time within the 40 minutes.
3. **"Order matters" (range-of-motion in wrong order): partly CONTRADICTED.** Handbook: "The skill task steps are not order dependent unless the words BEFORE or AFTER are used in a step." Do not tell readers every step is order-locked.
4. **Hand washing always first: CONSISTENT with a correction.** The first task is always one of four mandatory tasks (bedpan/urine output; catheter care on a manikin; gown and gloves with urinary drainage bag; perineal care on a manikin), each with "embedded hand washing" using soap and water. Handbook also says hand washing "must be demonstrated at the end of each" mandatory task. So "the first task always includes hand washing" is correct; "you start by washing hands" is not stated.
5. **Exam size and time: CONSISTENT.** Skills: 3 or 4 randomly selected tasks, 40 minutes (timers at 15 minutes left and at 40). Knowledge: 60 multiple-choice questions, 60 minutes, 76% or better to pass.
6. **Results show what you missed: CONSISTENT.** Handbook, Test Results: sign in to the TMU account at mc.tmutest.com; "Click View Failed Steps to see the steps missed." (Applies to the skill results screen.) D&S does not give results by phone.
7. **Challenge a result in MA: CONSISTENT (it exists, with strict limits).** Handbook, "Test Review Requests": you may "request a review of your test results or dispute any other testing condition." Call D&S first (888-401-0462, 8-8 ET Mon-Fri) to discuss the result, then submit the form on mc.tmutest.com. **$25 non-refundable deposit** (refunded only if the review finds in your favor). Request must be received **within 3 business days** of the official scoring; late requests are denied. D&S reviews the observer's notes and may interview the observer. Do not call it a "grievance."
8. Retakes: 4 knowledge attempts, 3 skills attempts; after that you must finish a new DPH-approved program. A no-show does not count as an attempt (handbook, Alternate Testing Languages section). Retake fees shown in the extracted text: knowledge $30, audio knowledge $40 ($30 + $10). The skills price did not extract from the table; the earlier brief says $70 from the 7.2024 handbook. `[Skills fee: confirm on the live D&S fee page before printing.]`
- **Quote option for C1** ("How you do stuff at work and how you do stuff when taking the state exam are two different things.") is fine as an opinion quote, because the handbook supports the point that the test scores listed steps only.

## C2. "Free" training, contracts, payback

1. **Federal rule 42 CFR 483.152(c): CONSISTENT.** Official eCFR text (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-483/subpart-D/section-483.152; read via the eCFR versioner API, date 2026-09-30) [OPENED]: "(c)(1) No nurse aide who is employed by, or who has received an offer of employment from, a facility on the date on which the aide begins a nurse aide training and competency evaluation program may be charged for any portion of the program (including any fees for textbooks or other required course materials)." "(2) If an individual who is not employed, or does not have an offer to be employed, as a nurse aide becomes employed by, or receives an offer of employment from, a facility not later than 12 months after completing a nurse aide training and competency evaluation program, the State must provide for the reimbursement of costs incurred in completing the program on a pro rata basis during the period in which the individual is employed as a nurse aide." Competency-evaluation counterpart at 483.154(c)(2)-(3) (same wording; seen in the Minnesota Dept. of Health federal-rule resource, https://www.health.mn.gov/facilities/providers/nursingassistant/docs/trainingresource.pdf, saved PDF text).
2. **Does it make a "repay if you quit" clause unenforceable? UNVERIFIABLE (no official source says so), and the search-summary claim is CONTRADICTED as worded.**
   - The regulation text bans charging the aide and says nothing about repayment or payback clauses (checked in the eCFR text).
   - The claim "you cannot be asked to pay the nursing home back if you leave before 12 months" mixes two things. The 12 months in the rule is the window in which a self-paying trainee who gets hired can be reimbursed by the State; it is not a minimum-stay period. **Drop that sentence.**
   - One state agency reads the rule strictly: Nebraska Medicaid Provider Bulletin 07-07 (2007) says if the facility employed or offered a job before course completion, "the provider is required to pay the full cost of the training program. The facility's obligation exists regardless of the length of time the aide remains employed" (https://dhhs.ne.gov/Medicaid%20Provider%20Bulletins/Provider%20Bulletin%2007-07.pdf, saved PDF text). That is Nebraska, not Massachusetts, and not CMS. Do not cite it for MA.
   - A search summary claimed the 1991 Federal Register preamble calls repayment "inappropriate." I could not open that preamble (56 FR 48919, Sept. 26, 1991, govinfo.gov) and no search result quoted it. `[SEARCH SUMMARY, unconfirmed; not usable]`
   - Careful scope note: the no-charge rule covers Medicare/Medicaid-certified nursing facilities and applies only if the aide is employed or has a job offer when training starts. It does not clearly cover a person who pays a school first, a hospital, a home care agency or an assisted living residence.
   - Recommendation: say "Federal rules say a nursing facility that has hired you or offered you a job before class starts cannot charge you for the training. Ask the facility, in writing, what happens if you leave. If you are told you owe money, ask the DPH Nurse Aide Registry (617-753-8144) or Mass Legal Help before signing." Do not say contracts are void or not enforced, and do not say they are enforceable.
3. **Massachusetts rule on training-repayment agreements: UNVERIFIABLE.** Searched: eCFR, Cornell 105 CMR 156.100/156.300 [OPENED, silent], mass.gov nurse aide pages (403), general search. No Massachusetts statute or DPH page on training repayment surfaced. Also not found: a DPH process for the 12-month reimbursement (content-strategy flagged the unenacted 2023 bill H.1216). Say "ask DPH."
4. **"Some facilities pay trainees an hourly wage during class": UNVERIFIABLE officially; fine as "some do" only if sourced to an example.** The federal 120-day / 4-month employment rule (C3 below) allows facilities to employ people while they train, so it is plausible. The MA workforce notice DCS INFO 14-258 (free four-week CNA program at a Lexington nursing facility) is a single, dated example `[SEARCH SUMMARY]`. Do not promise pay.
5. 4-month rule (relevant to "earn while you learn"): **CONSISTENT.** 42 CFR 483.35(d) (Cornell mirror [OPENED]): "A facility must not use any individual working in the facility as a nurse aide for more than 4 months, on a full-time basis, unless that individual is competent ... and has completed a training and competency evaluation program." The D&S handbook words it for MA as: "Federal and State regulations allow healthcare facilities to employ students for up to 120 days from the day employment and training is offered in an approved facility-based nurse aide training and competency evaluation program," and if you fail 4 knowledge or 3 skills attempts "the facility is no longer allowed to employ you to perform nurse aide duties." Use "up to 4 months (120 days)" and say it applies to nursing facilities.

## C3. First workplace, Care Compare, 3-star claim, staffing ratios

1. **Medicare Care Compare shows nursing home star ratings, staffing and inspections: CONSISTENT.** CMS Five-Star page [OPENED] https://www.cms.gov/medicare/health-safety-standards/certification-compliance/five-star-quality-rating-system : "There is one Overall 5-star rating for each nursing home, and separate ratings for health inspections, staffing and quality measures." The Care Compare tool itself (medicare.gov/care-compare) returned 403/empty; the search summary agrees that it is where the ratings are looked up. Cite the CMS page or the tool for readers; both are official. `[Care Compare page itself: SEARCH SUMMARY]`
2. **"Facilities rated below 3 stars cannot host clinicals": CONTRADICTED / drop.** The federal rule is not about stars. 42 CFR 483.151(b)(2) [Cornell, OPENED] bars approval of a nurse aide training program offered by a facility that in the previous two years was subject to an extended or partial extended survey, assessed a civil money penalty of $5,000 or more (adjusted yearly), or was subject to certain remedies. Nothing there or on the CMS Five-Star page links training to star ratings. The mass.gov summary says only that DPH-approved clinical sites must be "a level II or level III long-term care facility in good standing" `[SEARCH SUMMARY]`. Do not print the commenter's claim.
3. **No fixed CNA ratio: CONSISTENT for the federal rule; Massachusetts ratio UNVERIFIABLE.** 42 CFR 483.35(a) [OPENED]: facilities need "sufficient nursing staff ... to assure resident safety" based on "the number, acuity and diagnoses of the facility's resident population"; no numeric ratio is set. I did not find a MA aide-to-resident ratio on an official page (mass.gov blocked). Write "ask how many residents each aide has"; state no number.

## C10. English, dictionaries, translated exam

1. **Word-for-word dictionary allowed in MA: CONSISTENT.** May 2026 handbook: "LANGUAGE TRANSLATION DICTIONARIES: Published foreign word-for-word translation dictionaries are allowed. Dictionaries with definitions or handwriting/notes will not be allowed. You must show your published word-for-word translation dictionary to the test observer/proctor during check-in (on-site or remotely proctored)." Electronic dictionaries, translators and non-approved language translators are not allowed. (The California comment happens to match MA.)
2. **Does DPH or a program require a level of English? UNVERIFIABLE.** No English-proficiency rule in 105 CMR 156.100/156.300 [OPENED], the handbook, or search summaries of mass.gov. Do not say "no English needed" or "English required." Use: "Programs may set their own English level; ask. Some programs have an ESL track" (the Cambridge city program is a dated example from the strategy). NEPM (https://www.nepm.org/regional-news/2024-07-17/mass-now-offers-test-to-certify-nurse-aids-in-chinese-spanish-advocates-also-want-easier-english) [OPENED] reports the test vendor says the English exam is at a "6th grade reading level"; that is a news report of a vendor claim, so call it "reported," not a rule.
3. **Translated exam languages and Haitian Creole: CONSISTENT. Open item resolved.** May 2026 handbook (Alternate Testing Languages, updated Feb 19, 2026): "The nurse aide certification exam's knowledge and skills components are offered in Chinese (traditional and simplified), Haitian Creole, Spanish (simplified), and English." D&S home page [OPENED]: "Administration of exams in Haitian Creole will begin December 2, 2024." The Oct 2025 handbook language paragraph that omitted it is superseded. Rules to include: you must apply for the alternate language on the TMU page; you cannot switch back to English once you start; if you pass one part you cannot switch languages unless you use up your attempts and finish a new program. Alternate-language help line (855) 263-6050. Audio knowledge option exists ($10 extra, $40 total); federal rule 483.154(b)(1)(i) also says an aide may choose a written or oral exam (Minnesota federal-rule resource text).
4. **Is basic English enough for the job?** UNVERIFIABLE; community only. No official source.

### 1. The 87-hour rule
- **Rule exists: CONSISTENT `[SEARCH SUMMARY]`.** Multiple mass.gov search results (DPH Advisory Memo, https://www.mass.gov/info-details/dph-advisory-memo-new-certified-nurse-aide-cna-curriculum-framework ; news page https://www.mass.gov/news/new-certified-nurse-aide-cna-curriculum-framework) say: "DPH will be increasing the required minimum training hours from the federal standard of 75 hours to 87 hours, including 21 hours of supervised practical training." "DPH-approved training providers and education partners will be required to update curricula" and "Many approved programs already exceed this minimum training hour threshold." Detailed guidance "will be issued throughout 2026." Pages blocked; I could not open the memo.
- **Effective date: UNVERIFIABLE.** No summary gives a date from which programs must teach 87 hours. Memo date is shown as April 3, 4 or 6, 2026 in different results; say "April 2026."
