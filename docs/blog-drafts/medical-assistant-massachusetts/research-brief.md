## Licensing & Certification

### National view
- BLS: "Some states require that medical assistants graduate from an accredited program, be licensed or certified, or meet other prerequisites in order to practice." Most states do not require certification; employers may prefer it; some require Basic Life Support (BLS/CPR) certification. [OPENED] https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-assistants.htm
- BLS: workers without postsecondary education can learn on the job or through apprenticeships, with training lasting "several months" depending on the facility. [OPENED]

### Massachusetts (example state)
- **No state license or registration for medical assistants in Massachusetts.** Sources: (a) MGL c.112 s.265(c) says DPH must write regulations for the immunization law "but cannot license certified medical assistants" [OPENED] https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXVI/Chapter112/Section265 ; (b) a mass.gov summary says medical assistants are not licensed in Massachusetts [SEARCH SUMMARY, mass.gov doc "august-09-2018" and BORM material; I did not open the page]. A direct mass.gov statement "no license required" in one sentence: `[DATA NOT FOUND — searched: mass.gov BORM pages (403), mass.gov search (403)]`. Writer: say "Massachusetts does not license medical assistants" is supported by (a) plus summary (b); flag for one human check on mass.gov before publishing.
- **Delegation rule (what an MA may do):** The Board of Registration in Medicine rule 243 CMR 2.07(4) says: "There shall be no delegation of medical services to an individual who is not licensed to perform those services in Massachusetts." [OPENED, Cornell copy] https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/243-CMR-2-07 . It also says nothing in it permits an unauthorized person to do things that require a medical license. Heads up: this is the Board's rule for physicians; I did not find an official MA list of tasks MAs may perform. A plain reading is that Massachusetts is stricter than many states about MAs doing licensed tasks. Do not turn this into "MAs can never do X" beyond what is cited. Whether the rule has been amended since: `[DATA NOT FOUND — searched: law.cornell.edu (shows current text), mass.gov redline draft of 243 CMR 2.07-2.15 (search summary only, not opened)]`.
- **Injections (immunizations) exception, MGL c.112 s.265 [OPENED]:** A primary care provider may delegate giving an immunization to a "certified medical assistant," defined as someone who (i) graduated from a post-secondary medical assisting program accredited by CAAHEP (via its predecessor committee), ABHES, "or another certificate program that the commissioner of public health may approve"; (ii) is employed in the medical practice of a licensed primary care provider; and (iii) does basic administrative, clerical and clinical duties on the provider's specific authorization and under direct supervision. "Direct supervision" means the provider is present in the facility and immediately available, but need not be in the room. Key plain-language point: the statute's test is graduating from an accredited program and working for a primary care provider; it does not itself say you must pass the CMA exam. DPH circular letter DCP 17-8-102 (Aug 10, 2017) gives guidance on which training/certifications DPH accepts and says primary care providers must keep records to show the MA meets the rules. [SEARCH SUMMARY] https://www.mass.gov/doc/august-10-2017-circular-letter-immunization-administration-by-medical-assistants-0/download (403 to me; text not read directly). DPH also has a companion advisory for nurse practitioners/APRNs delegating immunizations to CMAs. [SEARCH SUMMARY] https://www.mass.gov/doc/ar-2001-advanced-practice-registered-nurse-delegation-of-the-administration-of-immunizations-to-certified-medical-assistants/download
- **Other injections, blood draws (venipuncture) in MA:** `[DATA NOT FOUND — searched: mass.gov BORM/DPH pages (403), 243 CMR 2.07 (no MA-specific text), malegislature.gov c.112 s.265 (immunizations only)]`. ### Clinical vs administrative duties
- BLS: medical assistants "complete administrative and clinical tasks, such as scheduling appointments and taking patients' vital signs"; they interview patients, measure vitals, assist with exams, give medications, collect specimens, and keep records and supplies. [OPENED] https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-assistants.htm
- O*NET splits it: clinical (vital signs, medical histories, preparing patients for exams, drawing blood and giving medications as allowed, collecting lab specimens, EKG/X-ray equipment) vs administrative (scheduling, medical records, billing/insurance coding, phones, paperwork). ## Training Paths (general industry — include duration and cost range)

- **Typical path per BLS:** postsecondary nondegree award (certificate or diploma) from community colleges, vocational schools or universities; programs take "about 1 or 2 years" and cover medical terminology, anatomy, pharmacology plus supervised practical experience. [OPENED] https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-assistants.htm
- **On-the-job alternative:** BLS says people can learn on the job or through apprenticeships; training lasts several months and is taught by physicians or experienced assistants. [OPENED] Note the tradeoff: this route will not make you eligible for the CMA (AAMA) (needs accredited program or approved apprenticeship) but can lead to RMA via work experience (3 years) or CCMA (1 to 2 years of supervised work).
- **How long (BLS):** 1 to 2 years for programs; several months on the job. Massachusetts examples from public colleges (official college pages) show shorter certificates exist:
  - Greenfield Community College: Medical Assistant Certificate, 29 credits, 8 months of coursework, accredited by CAAHEP (via MAERB), mostly online classes plus in-person labs; graduates are eligible for the CMA (AAMA), CCMA and RMA. [OPENED] https://www.gcc.mass.edu/academics/programs/medical-assistant/ . (A GCC news item says CAAHEP continuing accreditation was granted March 2025 [SEARCH SUMMARY].) Admission requires CORI/background check, physical exam and immunization forms, technical standards.
