Director's decisions, 2026-10-05. Inputs: `community-insights.md`, `community-verification.md`, `community-editorial.md`. Rules: Research Guidelines Part C (2+ independent voices or an official source; no CONTRADICTED claim; strategist KEEP; stricter call wins; if unsure, cut).

**Evidence is thin.** The researcher could not open Reddit or Quora; most forum items are search snippets. So: every item is a paraphrase, no quotes, soft wording ("some people say", "many people who started say"), no community numbers, no ratings. Facts inside items use the verified official figure.

## Corrections the writer must apply to the research brief
1. BLS says employers "may prefer or require" certification (not just "prefer").
2. Massachusetts law (MGL c.112 s.265) says nothing in the section "shall be construed as authorizing the licensure of certified medical assistants." Plain version: "No state license is required." The immunization rule (only graduates of an accredited program) is SEARCH SUMMARY for the mass.gov DPH letter but the statute text was opened on malegislature.gov. Put a VERIFY comment on the mass.gov-only facts.
3. Program accreditation: NECC and STCC state their medical assistant certificates are CAAHEP-accredited; MassBay is only "seeking" accreditation; Massasoit names only institutional accreditation; Quincy College does not state it. Do not say any of these leads to the CMA unless the page says so; and no school names in the post anyway.
4. NHA CCMA test plan and fee, AAMA fees and the AMCA passing score need a live check (VERIFY comments). Do not print the AMCA passing score.
5. Balance: the editorial notes warn the post leans critical. Keep four positives in: jobs are open (13% growth, about 109,700 openings a year), many medical assistants like their work, hybrid programs work, and there are concrete steps that help.
