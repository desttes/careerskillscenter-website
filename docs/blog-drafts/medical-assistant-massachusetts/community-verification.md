## Answers to the director's four specific questions

### Q1. Does the AAMA block people with a felony from the CMA? Do AMT (RMA) and NHA (CCMA) have similar rules?

**AAMA (CMA): Yes, a felony can block you, but it is not automatic and there is a waiver. CONSISTENT with the poster.**
- AAMA "Request for Waiver Form" (Rev 4-25) [OPENED, text extracted locally]: https://www.aama-ntl.org/docs/default-source/about-profession-and-credential/cma-exam/cma-aama-exam-request-waiver.pdf
  - Candidates who "have been found guilty of a felony or have pleaded guilty to a felony" (or had a professional license, registration or certification denied, revoked, suspended or put on probation) must check a box and file the waiver with their exam application.
  - Exact: "Adverse action taken against a professional credential, a felony conviction, or guilty plea does not automatically disqualify an individual from being eligible to sit for the CMA (AAMA) Certification Exam." AAMA's Certification Department reviews each case individually. If the incident has "a direct relationship to a potential violation(s) of the AAMA Code of Conduct," the Certifying Board "may impose sanctions, including barring a candidate from becoming certified ... either indefinitely or for a designated period."
  - To approve a waiver, mandatory: completing the sentence for the felony (or completing any sanctions against a professional credential). Exact: "Any and all drug-related convictions may render the candidate ineligible for waiver."
  - Required documents: written explanation (including age at the time and life changes since), official court and police documents, proof of completing probation/parole or treatment, proof sentence done and fines paid, and letters from people other than family and friends (employers, counselors, educators, probation officers, clergy) vouching for rehabilitation.
  - A granted waiver can be reviewed or pulled if the person has any later conviction or plea; the person must report it right away.
- The AAMA eligibility page itself says nothing about felonies [OPENED] https://www.aama-ntl.org/certification/eligibility . The rule sits in the waiver form and in the Code of Conduct and Disciplinary Standards PDF (https://www.aama-ntl.org/docs/default-source/about-profession-and-credential/cma-exam/aama-code-of-conduct-and-disciplinary-standards.pdf , could not be read; its content is described only via a search summary: possible sanctions include denial of eligibility, invalidated scores, probation, reprimand, temporary or permanent revocation) [SEARCH SUMMARY].
- Misdemeanors: a search summary of an AAMA blog (on a test.aama-ntl.org address) says the Certifying Board only considers felonies, not misdemeanors [SEARCH SUMMARY, not on a primary page I could read]. Do not state it as fact.

**AMT (RMA): Yes, similar. Case-by-case review; certain felonies may bar you.**
- AMT "Felony Information Checklist" [OPENED] https://americanmedtech.org/blog/blog-post/felony-information-checklist . Exact: "Even if a candidate meets certification eligibility criteria, AMT may determine that certain types of felony convictions in an applicant's background may preclude an applicant from sitting for an AMT certification examination." "Each application is reviewed carefully on a case-by-case basis by the AMT office before a determination is made." Contact: mail@americanmedtech.org, 847.823.5169.
- Search summary of the same family of pages (not opened in full) [SEARCH SUMMARY]: applicant sends date and nature of the felony, a personal statement and court documents in one submission; AMT answers within 30 days; an ineligible applicant may appeal; a felony may also bar recertification; and employers may refuse to hire a certified person with a felony.
- The AMT RMA program page (https://americanmedtech.org/medical-assistant) [OPENED] has no felony wording; the policy is on the checklist page.

**NHA (CCMA): UNVERIFIABLE.** I found no felony or criminal-record wording.
- NHA CCMA page [OPENED] https://www.nhanow.com/certification/nha-certifications/certified-clinical-medical-assistant-(ccma) : no mention of criminal background, felony or code of ethics. NHA's help page on exam requirements [OPENED] https://knowledge.nhanow.com/what-are-the-requirements-to-take-an-nha-exam covers other exams and says nothing on this for CCMA. The Candidate Handbook (https://www.nhanow.com/docs/default-source/test-plans/candidate_handbook.pdf) would not read as text.
- What a search summary of that handbook says [SEARCH SUMMARY]: NHA reserves the right to suspend or revoke a credential for unprofessional conduct, "particularly" unlawful activity that endangers others' safety or health; false eligibility information can void an exam or a credential. No stated felony bar for sitting the exam.
- Honest wording for the post: "AAMA and AMT will review a felony before they let you test; NHA does not publish a felony rule that I could find, so ask NHA." Do not say NHA has no rule.

**Other points on the poster's claim (C8).**
- "The CMA has stricter requirements" is CONTRADICTED in part: AMT has a similar felony review. Do not frame the felony issue as CMA-only.
- Practical note for readers with a record (opened, official): Greenfield CC's medical assistant page says "CORI (Criminal Offender Record Information) and/or additional background checks required for participation in this program" [OPENED] https://www.gcc.mass.edu/academics/programs/medical-assistant/ . Externship sites and employers may run background checks separately; the post should say "ask the program and the certifying body before you pay."
- Employers: AMT itself says some employers will not hire a felon even if certified [SEARCH SUMMARY, AMT checklist family].

### Q2. Can the NHA CCMA be earned without an externship?

**Yes, as written. CONSISTENT with C3.** [OPENED] https://www.nhanow.com/certification/nha-certifications/certified-clinical-medical-assistant-(ccma)
- Path 1: "Completed a medical assistant training or education program within the last 5 year[s]" (the page text reads "year"). No externship, hours or accreditation named.
- Path 2: high school diploma or GED/equivalency plus 1 year of supervised medical-assisting work in the last 3 years, or 2 years in the last 5.
- The handbook may define "program" more tightly; I could not read it. Caveat for the writer: say "the NHA page I read does not list an externship."
- Contrast, official: AMT RMA education route needs "720 hours of instruction that includes at least 160 hours of externship" [OPENED] https://americanmedtech.org/medical-assistant ; AAMA CMA needs a CAAHEP/ABHES-accredited program (brief, [OPENED] eligibility page). 
- Important nuance for C3: an exam with no externship rule does not mean employers skip hands-on skills. That part (employers rejecting online-only grads) is community opinion; no official source.
