# Editorial decisions: how to become a medical assistant (nationwide)

Director's calls, 2026-10-05, from `community-insights.md`, `community-verification.md` and `community-editorial.md`. Stricter call wins. Reddit/Quora content was snippet-only, so **no direct quotes are approved**. Every item is a paraphrase, framed as an experience, never as a fact. No usernames, thread links or site names. Never imply these people are Career Skills Center students.

Rules for the whole post:
- No MGMA percentages (the 56% May 2026 figure is unverified; MGMA is an industry group, not community). BLS already shows demand: 13% growth 2025-35, about 109,700 openings a year.
- No community pay figures and no "9 months" story.
- No invented experiences about AI, exam difficulty, class difficulty or the first weeks on the job (none were found). Handle those only with verified official facts.
- Balance: keep the positives next to the warnings: some people are hired right after training; demand is strong; paid routes exist; many people like the work; every warning comes with a step the reader can take.

## Verified figures the writer may use (all in research-brief.md and community-verification.md)
- AAMA Alternative Pathway: "currently available" (never "permanent"; it began as a 2019 pilot and no AAMA page says it is permanent).
- NHA CCMA fee: $169 (NHA store page). No retake fee found, so do not state one.
- Public college price examples: Anne Arundel (MD) $4,112 for county residents, 388 hours, 100-hour unpaid externship; Community College of Philadelphia non-credit $2,799, 180 hours, externship optional (start dates passed: say check current enrollment); Central Piedmont (NC) $5,376 to $14,400 estimated total expenses, diploma in about 2.5 semesters, CAAHEP accredited.
- Washington state: a state credential is required before working as a medical assistant (certified $145, registered $115; the registered credential is valid only with the same employer). Use in the "rules differ by state" box.
- Massachusetts immunization law (c.112 s.265): covers immunizations only, under direct supervision with the provider "immediately available"; a "primary care provider" is defined by function, so do not name doctors, NPs or PAs; the "certified medical assistant" definition is a graduate of an accredited program employed by the practice, so an on-the-job-only trainee does not fit. Say nothing about injections or blood draws beyond that (not found).
- Massachusetts programs: say Massasoit lists NECHE and Quincy/MassBay do not show CAAHEP, so never say these programs lead to the CMA (AAMA).
- Use BLS 13% and 109,700 openings, not MGMA's older 15% to 2033.
