## C1. First job and hiring gap

(1) MGMA 47% (May 2025, n=420): CONSISTENT. Source: https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/why-medical-assistants-are-still-tougher-to-hire-today-than-nurses-coders-and-other-medical-practice-staff . The page says a May 6, 2025 poll found 47% of practice leaders named medical assistants the hardest role to recruit (nurses 15%, billers 10%, coders 9%), 420 applicable responses. It also says practices are "hiring less experienced candidates and providing internal training" and that bigger systems pull MAs away with higher pay and sign-on bonuses.
- 56% (May 2026): UNVERIFIABLE. I did not open the May 2026 poll page (the community file itself only had a search snippet). Do not print it.
- MGMA is an industry association, not government. Director decides. If used, keep it soft ("clinics say medical assistants are hard to find").
- Out of date: the same MGMA page cites BLS "15% through 2033, 119,800 openings". The current BLS figure is 13% for 2025 to 2035, about 109,700 openings a year (https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-assistants.htm). Never copy MGMA's BLS number.
(2) "Took 9 months": UNVERIFIABLE (one person's story). Agree with the note: do not print.
- Supporting official fact for the gap: BLS lists typical entry education as a postsecondary nondegree award, with some employers hiring high school graduates and training them on the job (same BLS URL).

## C2. Hired with no training, later told to certify

(1) CCMA experience route: CONSISTENT, with an addition. NHA page https://www.nhanow.com/certification/nha-certifications/certified-clinical-medical-assistant-(ccma) says: high school diploma or GED plus EITHER an MA program completed within the last 5 years OR "1 year of supervised work experience in a medical assisting field within the last 3 years; or 2 years ... within the last 5 years". The brief's "1 year in last 3, or 2 in last 5" matches.
- RMA experience route: CONSISTENT. AMT (https://americanmedtech.org/Medical-Assistant): 3 years full-time at 36 hours a week within the past 7 years (5,616 hours), covering clinical and administrative duties; needs high school diploma or GED, an employment form and a valid CPR card.
- CMA (AAMA) has NO work-experience-only route. Its four routes are an accredited program, previous certification, medical assisting educator, or the Alternative Pathway (https://aama-ntl.org/certification/eligibility). An untrained hire cannot reach the CMA (AAMA) that way. Worth saying in the post.
(2) "Certify within 6 months of hire": UNVERIFIABLE on a live official page. Search results show Novant Health postings (NC and SC) saying a non-certified hire must certify by the end of the sixth month and sign an education agreement. [SEARCH SUMMARY] The icims posting pages I opened were expired ("filled or no longer available"), and Saint Luke's returned HTTP 530. If the writer wants the claim, soften it to "some employers give you a set time, such as six months, to get certified."
- Rule check: Washington requires a state credential before you work as a medical assistant (see Strategy open item 5), so the "start with no certificate" route is closed or limited in some states. Add a state-varies line.

## C6. Why people leave, why they stay

No printable facts per the file. One check: Annals of Family Medicine abstract (https://www.annfammed.org/content/21/Supplement_1/4013): exploratory mixed-methods study, 350 medical assistants from Colorado, New Mexico, Utah and Arizona, surveyed through AAMA membership, published January 2023. Support from coworkers and the organization predicted higher satisfaction and lower burnout; heavy workload and lack of recognition hurt. Mean satisfaction was 5.9 of 7, burnout 2.4 of 5.
- CORRECTION: it is a four-state sample, not a national "US study." Do not call it nationwide.
- Out of date: no. The PayScale 3.7/5 rating is a salary aggregator, not an allowed source. Do not use.

## C7. Nursing plans and credit transfer

(1) "MA credits don't transfer to nursing": CONSISTENT with "it depends on the school," CONTRADICTED as a blanket statement. Lakeland Community College (Ohio, public) says: "College level, general education courses may transfer. Technical courses in medical assisting may not transfer to every institution," and tells students to ask the target school (https://lakelandcc.edu/web/about/curriculum-transfer-medical-assisting). (2) MGMA tuition-assistance line: CONSISTENT. MGMA May 2025 page lists "offering tuition assistance for MAs pursuing nursing degrees" among retention strategies. Industry source, soft use only.

## C8. Online-only programs

CONSISTENT.
- CCMA does not require an externship to sit the exam. NHA's route 1 asks for completing a training or education program within the last 5 years, or route 2 work experience (https://www.nhanow.com/certification/nha-certifications/certified-clinical-medical-assistant-(ccma)). The page names no externship minimum. So a person with an online-only course can sit for it. [Exact program-content rules are in the Candidate Handbook, which I could not open.]
- AAMA Alternative Pathway (https://www.aama-ntl.org/certification/eligibility/alternative-pathway): at least 560 academic contact hours (not counting practicum), a 160-hour practicum or externship (or 1,000 hours of medical assisting experience after the program), "at least 10" injections and "a minimum of 10" phlebotomy procedures demonstrated. Program must be at an institution accredited by a USDE- or CHEA-recognized body. Exam fee $125 for AAMA members, $250 for nonmembers; documentation review is free. CONSISTENT with the brief's 10 and 10.
- Also note: the Kaiser WA, Washington MA-Certified rules and RMA education route all require a 160-hour externship, so online-only is a real risk.

## C9. CMA vs RMA vs CCMA

CONSISTENT. RMA is a national credential from American Medical Technologists, not state-bound (https://americanmedtech.org/Medical-Assistant): routes are education (720 hours including 160 hours externship, within 4 years), competency route (720, or 500 hours with 160 externship, plus competency check and CPR), work experience (3 years in last 7), military, instructor. Application fee $150 (includes application, exam and first annual fee). So the allnurses line that the RMA is "registered only in the state that you currently live" is CONTRADICTED. Matches the file's own note.
- "Many postings list CCMA, CMA or RMA as equal" : [SEARCH SUMMARY], not opened. Novant Health postings list CMA (AAMA), CCMA (NHA), RMA (AMT) and others as accepted. Consistent in spirit but not opened.
- Washington accepts five exams for its MA-Certified credential (see item 5), another sign that states treat them as equal.

### (5) Washington state MA credential rule
Washington requires a state credential before you work as a medical assistant. Source: Washington DOH, https://doh.wa.gov/licenses-permits-and-certificates/professions-new-renew-or-update/medical-assistant/credentialing-information : the state recognizes MA-Registered, MA-Certified, MA-Hemodialysis Technician, MA-Phlebotomist and MA-Certified EMT, and credentials must be obtained before practice.
- **MA-Certified** (https://doh.wa.gov/licenses-permits-and-certificates/professions-new-renew-or-update/medical-assistant/credentialing-requirements): high school diploma or equivalent; completion of an ABHES- or CAAHEP-accredited MA program, OR a regionally or nationally accredited program of at least 720 clock hours including a 160-plus-hour externship, OR a Washington registered apprenticeship, OR military training deemed equivalent; AND pass one of five exams within 5 years before applying: AAMA CMA, AMT RMA, NHA CCMA, NCCT NCMA, or AMCA. Interim certification: available if you meet everything except the exam; it expires when you pass or after one year, whichever comes first. Initial application fee $145.
- **MA-Registered** (initial fee $115): a health care practitioner attests to your competency for listed tasks; the endorsement is valid only while you stay employed by that same practitioner, clinic or group practice. This is the on-the-job route.
- Fees, "non-refundable" per WAC 246-12-340; online applicants pay an extra convenience fee.
- Cite for Part: this is one of few states with a state-issued MA credential. For most states there is none (BLS: "some states require" graduating from an accredited program, licensing or certification, https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-assistants.htm).

### (6) Massachusetts immunization-delegation wording (M.G.L. c.112 s.265)
Source (opened 2026-10-05): https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXVI/Chapter112/Section265 , titled "Administration of immunization by certified medical assistant."
- Delegation: "a primary care provider acting within the primary care provider's designated scope of practice may delegate the administration of an immunization of a patient to a certified medical assistant."
- Who counts as a "certified medical assistant" (all three parts): (i) graduated from a post-secondary medical assisting program accredited by CAAHEP (AMA committee), ABHES, or another certificate program the Commissioner of Public Health may approve; (ii) employed in the medical practice of a licensed primary care provider; (iii) performs basic administrative, clerical and clinical duties on specific authorization and under direct supervision of a licensed primary care provider.
- "Primary care provider": "a health care professional qualified to provide general medical care for common health care problems who: (i) supervises, coordinates, prescribes or otherwise provides or proposes health care services; (ii) initiates referrals for specialist care; and (iii) maintains continuity of care within the health care professional's scope of practice." The statute does NOT name physicians, nurse practitioners or physician assistants; the definition is by function. The statute does not say which professions qualify, so do not claim NPs or PAs count. Say "a primary care provider as the law defines it."
- Direct supervision: the provider is in the facility and "immediately available" throughout the procedure, but need not be in the room.
- Scope: it covers immunizations only; the section says nothing about other injections or blood draws. It also says: "Nothing in this section shall be construed as authorizing the licensure of certified medical assistants." DPH must write regulations. I did not search for those DPH regulations this round [not searched].
- Implication for the post: in Massachusetts the law that lets an MA give shots speaks only to immunizations, and the statute's definition of "certified medical assistant" relies on graduating from an accredited program, not on passing CMA, CCMA or RMA. Someone trained only on the job does not fit that definition. This is a Massachusetts section point, not a nationwide one.
