Healthcare Careers in Massachusetts: Jobs, Pay and How to Train.

Healthcare is one of the largest and steadiest parts of the Massachusetts economy. Many roles don’t need a four-year degree, and several can be trained for in months. Here’s what the jobs pay, how to train, and how to get help paying for it.

Check Your Options

The field in Massachusetts

Is healthcare right for you?

Healthcare isn’t only doctors and nurses. Behind every clinic and hospital is a team of billers, coders, assistants, aides and technicians — roles you can often train for quickly. This field suits people who are reliable, detail-oriented, and want steady work that helps others. Some jobs are hands-on with patients; others are office-based and can be done partly or fully online.

Roles & pay

Common healthcare roles in Massachusetts

Pay figures are the Massachusetts median annual wage from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (OEWS, May 2025). Half of workers earn more, half earn less.

Medical Billing & Coding

Turn doctor visits into standard codes and insurance claims. Detailed, office-based work, often remote once you have experience.

Massachusetts median pay
$60,350 a year
BLS OEWS, May 2025 · SOC 29-2072
Typical training
Short certificate or online course, often a few months. Employers value a voluntary AAPC (CPC/CPB) or AHIMA (CCA) certification.
MA license / certification
No Massachusetts license. Certification is voluntary but employer-preferred.
Can you train online?
Yes — one of the most online-friendly healthcare paths.

Medical Assistant

Work alongside doctors and nurses: rooming patients, taking vital signs, and handling front-office tasks.

Massachusetts median pay
$49,460 a year
BLS OEWS, May 2025 · SOC 31-9092
Typical training
Postsecondary certificate, often under a year. A voluntary CMA (AAMA) or RMA (AMT) certification helps.
MA license / certification
No Massachusetts license; certification is voluntary.
Can you train online?
Partly. Classroom work can be online, but you need an in-person clinical externship.

Phlebotomy Technician

Draw blood for tests, donations and research. A focused role you can train for quickly.

Massachusetts median pay
$50,170 a year
BLS OEWS, May 2025 · SOC 31-9097
Typical training
Short certificate plus supervised, hands-on draws. Many earn a voluntary certification (e.g., ASCP or NHA).
MA license / certification
No Massachusetts license; national certification is voluntary.
Can you train online?
Partly. Theory can be online, but you must practice real blood draws in person.

Pharmacy Technician

Help pharmacists prepare and dispense medications in pharmacies and hospitals.

Massachusetts median pay
$46,470 a year
BLS OEWS, May 2025 · SOC 29-2052
Typical training
Short training program. Many earn PTCB or ExCPT certification.
MA license / certification
Massachusetts requires a Pharmacy Technician license from the Board of Registration in Pharmacy — even if you’re nationally certified — and you must be 18 or older. (mass.gov)
Can you train online?
Partly. Coursework can be online; you gain hands-on experience on the job.

EKG Technician

Run electrocardiogram (EKG/ECG) tests that record the heart’s activity for doctors to read.

Massachusetts median pay
$105,580 a year (This is the BLS median for the broad ‘cardiovascular technologists and technicians’ group; EKG-only roles typically pay well below this.)
BLS OEWS, May 2025 · SOC 29-2031
Typical training
Short certificate plus hands-on practice. A voluntary CET certification (CCI or NHA) can help.
MA license / certification
No Massachusetts license; certification is voluntary.
Can you train online?
Partly. Theory can be online, but you need hands-on practice with the equipment.

Nurse Aide (CNA)

Provide hands-on daily care to patients and residents in nursing homes, hospitals and home care.

Massachusetts median pay
$46,680 a year
BLS OEWS, May 2025 · SOC 31-1131
Typical training
A Department of Public Health–approved Nurse Aide Training Program plus the state competency exam.
MA license / certification
Massachusetts requires completing a DPH-approved training program and passing the state competency evaluation to be listed on the Nurse Aide Registry. (mass.gov, DPH)
Can you train online?
Partly. Some classroom hours may be online, but clinical hours must be done in person.

Home Health Aide

Help older adults and people with disabilities live safely and independently at home.

Massachusetts median pay
$40,910 a year
BLS OEWS, May 2025 · SOC 31-1120
Typical training
Training is usually provided by the employing agency; Medicare-certified agencies follow federal training standards.
MA license / certification
No Massachusetts license for the role; agencies set training requirements.
Can you train online?
Partly. Some training is online, but hands-on care skills are practiced in person.

Medical Administrative Assistant

Run the front office of a clinic or practice: scheduling, records, insurance and patient intake.

Massachusetts median pay
$50,290 a year
BLS OEWS, May 2025 · SOC 43-6013
Typical training
Short certificate. A voluntary CMAA certification can help.
MA license / certification
No Massachusetts license; certification is voluntary.
Can you train online?
Yes — this office-based role is well suited to online training.

Choosing a path

Pick your path by where you want to work

Healthcare roles differ less by pay than by the setting and the kind of day you want. A good first question isn’t “which pays most?” but “where do I picture myself working?”

In someone’s home or the community

One-on-one caregiving in people’s homes — helping older adults and people with disabilities live safely and independently. Personal, relationship-based work, often on a schedule that fits your life.

Roles: Home Health Aide, CNA (home care)

In a hospital or nursing facility

Fast-paced, hands-on work with many patients across a shift. A good fit if you want to be on your feet, part of a care team, and in the middle of things.

Roles: CNA, Phlebotomy Technician, EKG Technician

In a clinic or doctor’s office

A mix of patient contact and front-office work — rooming patients, taking vital signs, scheduling and records — usually on regular daytime hours.

Roles: Medical Assistant, Medical Administrative Assistant, Phlebotomy

Behind the scenes, often from home

Detail work with records, codes and insurance claims, with little or no patient contact — the most remote-friendly healthcare path once you have some experience.

Roles: Medical Billing & Coding, Medical Administrative Assistant

License vs. certification — an important difference. Two of these roles need the state’s approval before you can work: nurse aides (CNAs) must finish a Department of Public Health–approved training program and pass the state competency exam to be listed on the Nurse Aide Registry, and pharmacy technicians must be licensed by the Massachusetts Board of Registration in Pharmacy. The rest — billing and coding, medical assistant, phlebotomy, EKG and medical admin — have no state license. For those, a voluntary national certification (for example AAPC CPC/CPB or AHIMA CCA for coding, AAMA CMA or AMT RMA for medical assistants, or ASCP or NHA for phlebotomy) isn’t required by law, but it’s what many employers look for.

An honest word on pay. The Massachusetts medians above include experienced workers; beginners earn less. Nationally, the lowest-paid 10% of nursing assistants earn under $33,940, phlebotomists under $35,780, medical assistants under $36,050, and medical records specialists under $37,000 (BLS, May 2025). These are short, postsecondary certificate paths — a nursing assistant, for example, finishes a state-approved program and a competency exam, not a college degree (BLS) — so pay grows with experience and with stepping up to higher roles or further nursing training.

Paying for it

How to pay for training in Massachusetts

Most people combine more than one source. Massachusetts residents who are unemployed, laid off, or working in a low-wage job may qualify for state-funded training through a MassHire career center. If you get unemployment benefits, ask about Section 30. Employers can be reimbursed for training their staff through the state Workforce Training Fund. Only a MassHire career center can approve funding — we’ll help you understand your options.

Your next step

Start with the state

Massachusetts runs the system that pays for most career training. Two free steps get you moving toward a healthcare career:

  1. Find your MassHire Career Center. See the list of locations and contact the one nearest you.
  2. Register on JobQuest. You need a JobQuest account before you can get training funding. Register at jobquest.mass.gov, then ask about a Training Information Meeting.

From there, a career counselor helps you check your eligibility and pick a state-approved training program in this field.

Healthcare careers FAQ

Do I need a college degree to work in healthcare in Massachusetts?

No, not for many roles. Jobs like medical billing and coding, medical assistant, phlebotomy technician, nurse aide (CNA) and home health aide are open to people without a four-year degree, and several can be trained for in a matter of months.

Which healthcare jobs can I train for online?

Office-based roles like medical billing and coding and medical administrative assistant are the most online-friendly. Hands-on roles such as medical assistant, phlebotomy, nurse aide and EKG technician can start online but require in-person clinical practice.

Do I need a license to work in healthcare in Massachusetts?

It depends on the role. Nurse aides (CNAs) must complete a DPH-approved training program and pass the state competency exam, and pharmacy technicians must be licensed by the Board of Registration in Pharmacy. Roles like medical billing and coding, medical assistant and phlebotomy have no state license; employers often prefer a voluntary national certification.

How much do entry-level healthcare jobs pay in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts median pay ranges from about $40,910 a year for home health aides to about $60,350 for medical records (billing and coding) specialists, according to BLS OEWS data for May 2025.

How do I pay for healthcare training?

Massachusetts residents may qualify for state-funded training through a MassHire career center. If you get unemployment benefits, ask about Section 30. Start with the free job training guide and check your options.
